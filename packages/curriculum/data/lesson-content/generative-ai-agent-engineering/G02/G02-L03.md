# Persona prompting, reasoning prompts and prompt injection

**G02-L03 · Intermediate · 105 min including practice**

## Learning objective

Use role/context and concise reasoning artifacts without treating reasoning text as proof.

## Concept notes

Persona prompts establish tone, audience and constraints, not genuine expertise. Chain-of-thought prompting is a research approach to eliciting intermediate reasoning; applications can instead request concise justification, verifiable calculations or structured plans. Untrusted content must remain data.

## Worked scenario

Ask a tutor to return an answer plus a short evidence-based explanation, then test adversarial user text.

## Common mistake

Trusting a plausible explanation as evidence of correctness or exposing hidden reasoning or secrets.

## Practice exercise

Compare persona vs neutral prompts and test input that asks to ignore instructions.

## Deeper theory and design

Persona prompts specify tone, role and audience, not real authority or identity. Reasoning prompts can request concise explanations, verifiable intermediate results or use of tools; hidden chain-of-thought is not required or necessarily faithful. Treat retrieved and user-provided content as untrusted data.

## Debugging clinic

A persona says it may ignore access policy; enforce permission in application code, not a prompt claim.
