## D04-L01 — Transfer learning, embeddings and self-supervised learning

### Core concept

Transfer learning adapts a pretrained model through frozen feature extraction or fine-tuning. Self-supervised learning constructs training signals from raw data, such as masked tokens or contrastive views. Embeddings map items into a representation space.

### Theory and reasoning

Transfer learning reuses representations learned on prior data; frozen backbone reduces compute and overfit risk, partial unfreeze adapts to domain shift. Self-supervised objectives derive targets from input itself, e.g. masked reconstruction or contrastive views.

### Worked scenario

Freeze a backbone, train a classification head, then selectively unfreeze layers.

### Design and verification

Frozen parameters do not guarantee batch-norm running stats stay fixed; set frozen modules to eval if required.

### Practice

## D04-L02 — Autoencoders, VAEs and GANs

### Core concept

Autoencoders reconstruct inputs through a latent representation; VAEs learn probabilistic latents with reconstruction and KL terms. GANs train a generator against a discriminator in an adversarial objective.

### Theory and reasoning

Autoencoders reconstruct input through bottleneck. VAE learns a distribution q(z given x), samples via reparameterization and balances reconstruction with KL regularization. GAN uses competing generator/discriminator objectives, not a reconstruction requirement.

### Worked scenario

Train a small autoencoder and inspect reconstruction error and latent structure.

### Design and verification

Good reconstruction does not ensure useful generation or anomaly detection; validate shift cases and avoid interpreting reconstruction score as calibrated probability.

### Practice

## D04-L03 — Diffusion and score-based generative models

### Core concept

Diffusion models corrupt data with noise according to a schedule and learn a reverse process or equivalent prediction target. Sampling iteratively denoises; conditioning guides generation. Latent diffusion operates in a learned compressed space.

### Theory and reasoning

Forward diffusion progressively perturbs clean data; q(x_t given x_0)=√ᾱ_t x_0+√(1−ᾱ_t)ε. Denoising models predict noise or related targets conditioned on t; reverse sampling iterates a scheduler. Different timesteps change signal-to-noise.

### Worked scenario

Add Gaussian noise at several steps and train a toy noise predictor.

### Design and verification

Confusing α_t and cumulative ᾱ_t mis-scales noise; check endpoints and use scheduler-consistent prediction parameterization.

### Practice

Visualize a simple noising schedule and compare denoising errors across timesteps.

Acceptance: Explain the trade-off between sampling compute and quality.

## D04-L04 — Reinforcement learning and preference optimization

### Core concept

An agent interacts with an environment to optimize expected return. Value functions estimate future return; policy gradients optimize a parameterized policy. RLHF may use learned reward models; DPO directly optimizes preferences under its assumptions.

### Theory and reasoning

A bandit chooses actions without modeling state transitions; RL adds sequential dynamics and returns. Preference optimization tunes policy behavior from comparisons; RLHF and DPO are different approaches. Exploration collects information at opportunity cost.

### Worked scenario

Compare random and epsilon-greedy policies on a toy bandit; relate preferences to language-model adaptation.

### Design and verification

Evaluating one seed can reward lucky initialization; average across seeds and distinguish observed reward from expected regret.

### Practice

Implement a two-armed bandit and report cumulative regret across seeds.
