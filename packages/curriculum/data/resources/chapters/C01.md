## C01-L01 — Image representation, color spaces and OpenCV

### Core concept
Images are arrays of pixels with spatial dimensions and channels. OpenCV commonly reads color images in BGR order; RGB, grayscale and HSV serve different tasks. Data range and dtype affect arithmetic.

### Theory and reasoning
An image is an array with spatial and channel dimensions; dtype determines representable range. OpenCV commonly uses BGR, while many plotting/model tools expect RGB. HSV separates hue from saturation/value but hue wraparound matters.

### Worked scenario
Convert a BGR image to grayscale and HSV; inspect channel histograms.

### Design and verification
Displaying BGR as RGB swaps red and blue. Convert at library boundaries and document normalization ranges.

### Practice
Create a synthetic color image, convert spaces and verify channel values.

Acceptance: Shapes and dtype are recorded; channel conversion is correct.

## C01-L02 — Filtering, edges and morphology

### Core concept
Convolution filters alter local neighborhoods. Gaussian blur reduces high-frequency noise; Sobel and Canny detect edges; erosion and dilation change binary regions. Opening removes small foreground structures, closing fills small gaps.

### Theory and reasoning
Convolutional filtering smooths or emphasizes local variation; Canny detects edges after smoothing/gradient processing. Morphological erosion shrinks foreground, dilation expands it; opening removes isolated bright pixels, closing fills small gaps.

### Worked scenario
Clean a noisy binary mask and detect edges around a shape.

### Design and verification
Large erosion removes thin defects; evaluate object retention and noise reduction against annotated examples.

### Practice
Compare masks for multiple kernel sizes and quantify foreground area.

Acceptance: Preserve target shape while removing isolated noise.

## C01-L03 — Geometric transforms, calibration and features

### Core concept
Affine transforms preserve parallelism; homographies model planar projective mappings. Camera intrinsics and distortion parameters support calibration. ORB detects and describes local features for matching.

### Theory and reasoning
Affine transforms preserve parallelism; homographies model perspective mapping between planes. Feature matching pairs descriptors, and RANSAC estimates geometry robustly under outliers. Reprojection error compares transformed points with observed correspondences.

### Worked scenario
Match features between a synthetic image and a rotated version, then estimate a homography with RANSAC.

### Design and verification
A homography fitted to four points exactly may be wrong for nonplanar scenes; inspect additional correspondences and camera geometry.

### Practice
Apply a perspective transform and quantify inlier matches or reprojection error.

Acceptance: Use robust estimation and report geometric assumptions.
