## M03-L01 — Classification, regression and ranking metrics

### Core concept
Precision measures positive prediction correctness; recall measures positive coverage. ROC-AUC evaluates ranking across thresholds, while PR curves highlight rare-positive trade-offs. MAE and RMSE measure regression errors; nDCG rewards relevant ranked results near the top.

### Theory and reasoning
Precision=TP/(TP+FP), recall=TP/(TP+FN), F1 harmonizes them. MAE is absolute error; RMSE emphasizes outliers. Ranking metrics such as recall@k and NDCG@k evaluate top results, not score calibration.

### Worked scenario
Compare two fraud systems with equal accuracy but different minority recall.

### Design and verification
Accuracy .97 masks rare-event errors; inspect confusion counts and business capacity rather than reporting accuracy alone.

### Practice

Acceptance: Metric choice matches business costs and includes subgroup analysis.

## M03-L02 — Cross-validation and hyperparameter search

### Core concept
Cross-validation rotates validation folds; grouped or time-aware variants respect dependencies. Grid/random search explores parameter configurations; nested CV separates tuning from performance estimation. Bayesian optimization chooses promising candidates adaptively.

### Theory and reasoning
Cross-validation estimates variability across data splits. Hyperparameter search consumes validation information, so selection score is optimistic. Nested CV or independent holdout evaluates the selected procedure. Randomized search can cover continuous spaces efficiently.

### Worked scenario
Tune forest depth and leaf size with grouped folds.

### Design and verification
Search iterations increase after seeing test performance. Retire that test as selection data and obtain a fresh holdout.

### Practice

## M03-L03 — Imbalanced learning, calibration and uncertainty

### Core concept
Class weights alter training loss; resampling changes the training distribution. Calibration compares predicted probability with observed frequency and can use held-out sigmoid or isotonic fitting. Prediction intervals or conformal methods quantify uncertainty under stated assumptions.

### Theory and reasoning
Ranking quality differs from calibration: a calibrated score of .2 should correspond to roughly 20% positives in comparable examples. Brier score averages squared probability error. Resampling changes training prevalence and may distort probabilities.

### Worked scenario
Use class weighting for rare events, then compare reliability diagrams before and after calibration.

### Design and verification
Oversampling before split duplicates positives into holdout; resample inside training only and do not change test prevalence.

### Practice

Acceptance: Report threshold, prevalence and calibration approach.

## M03-L04 — Interpretability, fairness and error analysis

### Core concept
Permutation importance measures score changes when a feature is shuffled; SHAP attributes model output relative to a baseline. Explanations are not causal evidence. Fairness metrics can conflict, so choose context-appropriate definitions and examine subgroups.

### Theory and reasoning
Local attribution explains model output under a chosen feature perturbation/reference, not causal effect. Fairness metrics depend on task and protected-group definitions; equalized odds and calibration may conflict. Slice estimates need uncertainty and adequate sample sizes.

### Worked scenario
Compare recall and false-positive rate across relevant consented groups and investigate proxy features.

### Design and verification
A subgroup with two examples has 100% error; report support and intervals, avoid broad claims or targeting based on unstable estimates.

### Practice
Produce an error slice report with subgroup sizes, metrics and limitations.

Acceptance: Small groups are flagged; proposed mitigations are evaluated on validation data.
