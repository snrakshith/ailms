## D01-L01 — Tensors, autograd and PyTorch fundamentals

### Core concept
Tensors have shape, dtype and device. Autograd records differentiable operations to compute gradients. Gradients accumulate by default and must be cleared between optimizer updates.

### Theory and reasoning
Tensors have shape, dtype and device. Autograd records operations on tensors requiring gradients, then applies reverse-mode differentiation to scalar loss. Gradients accumulate unless cleared; non-differentiable operations may break the graph.

### Worked scenario
Differentiate y=(x**2).sum() for a vector with requires_grad enabled.

### Design and verification
Calling `.detach()` or converting to NumPy before loss disconnects the gradient; inspect requires_grad and grad_fn.

### Practice
Compute an analytic gradient and compare with autograd.

Acceptance: Gradient equals 2*x within tolerance.

## D01-L02 — Perceptrons, MLPs and activation functions

### Core concept
An affine layer maps x to Wx+b. Nonlinear activations such as ReLU or GELU allow composition to model nonlinear functions. Without nonlinearities, stacked affine layers remain affine.

### Theory and reasoning
A linear classifier cannot separate XOR because no line separates opposite corners. Hidden nonlinear layers create new representations; without activations stacked linear layers collapse into one linear transform.

### Worked scenario
Train a two-layer MLP on XOR or a small tabular classification dataset.

### Design and verification
Applying sigmoid before BCEWithLogitsLoss applies sigmoid twice. Feed raw logits and threshold only for evaluation.

### Practice
Compare a linear model with an MLP on XOR.

Acceptance: MLP can fit XOR; linear baseline cannot perfectly separate it.

## D01-L03 — Forward pass, backpropagation and loss functions

### Core concept
Forward propagation produces predictions and loss; backpropagation applies the chain rule to compute parameter gradients. Cross entropy for multiclass logits, BCE for binary and MSE for regression require appropriate target formats.

### Theory and reasoning
For ŷ=wx+b and loss (ŷ−y)², chain rule yields ∂L/∂w=2(ŷ−y)x and ∂L/∂b=2(ŷ−y). Backprop efficiently reuses intermediate derivatives rather than perturbing each weight.

### Worked scenario
Compute gradients for a tiny scalar two-layer network and compare to autograd.

### Design and verification
Mistaking sum for mean loss changes effective step scale with batch size. Match reduction in manual derivation.

### Practice
Hand-derive gradients for a scalar linear prediction and squared loss.

Acceptance: Analytic and autograd gradients agree.

## D01-L04 — Optimizers, initialization and learning-rate schedules

### Core concept
SGD updates using minibatch gradients; momentum smooths updates; AdamW adapts coordinate step sizes with decoupled weight decay. Initialization and schedules influence optimization stability and convergence.

### Theory and reasoning
SGD follows the current gradient; momentum accumulates velocity; Adam adapts coordinate steps via moment estimates. Initialization affects activation/gradient variance; schedules reduce or vary step sizes. Comparing optimizers requires identical data and budgets.

### Worked scenario
Compare AdamW and SGD on the same architecture and seed budget.

### Design and verification
Reusing optimizer moments across trials invalidates comparison; initialize weights and optimizer consistently.

### Practice
Train short runs at several learning rates and log loss curves.

Acceptance: Choose based on validation and stability rather than a single training curve.
