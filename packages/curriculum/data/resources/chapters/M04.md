## M04-L01 — Clustering: k-means, hierarchical and DBSCAN

### Core concept

k-means minimizes squared distance to centroids; hierarchical clustering builds nested groups; DBSCAN uses density connectivity and can mark noise. Scale and geometry strongly affect results.

### Theory and reasoning

k-means optimizes squared distance to centroids, assuming compact groups. Hierarchical clustering constructs a linkage tree; DBSCAN groups density-connected points and marks noise. Silhouette compares intra-cluster cohesion and nearest-cluster separation, but can favor convex groups.

### Worked scenario

Cluster synthetic crescent-shaped data using k-means and DBSCAN.

### Design and verification

Treating DBSCAN noise as an ordinary cluster distorts silhouette. Report noise rate and explain score calculation.

### Practice

## M04-L02 — Dimensionality reduction: PCA, t-SNE and UMAP

### Core concept

PCA is a linear projection; t-SNE and UMAP construct nonlinear embeddings emphasizing aspects of neighborhood structure. Low-dimensional plots may distort global distances, density and cluster separation.

### Theory and reasoning

PCA gives orthogonal linear directions of maximum variance. t-SNE emphasizes local neighborhoods and may distort global distances; UMAP approximates a neighborhood graph embedding. Neither nonlinear visualization alone validates cluster labels.

### Worked scenario

Reduce handwritten digit vectors to two dimensions and compare stability across seeds.

### Design and verification

Interpreting distances between t-SNE islands as real distances is unjustified; check original-space neighbors and repeat seeds.

### Practice

## M04-L03 — Anomaly detection and novelty detection

### Core concept

Isolation Forest isolates observations via random splits; one-class methods estimate a normal region. Anomaly scores require thresholds and operational review; anomalies may be benign or reflect data quality.

### Theory and reasoning

Anomaly detection flags unusual observations relative to training distribution; novelty detection assumes relatively clean reference data. A score is not a probability of fraud. Fixed alert capacity is an operational constraint.

### Worked scenario

Detect rare sensor spikes with a model trained on normal historical data.

### Design and verification

Training on contaminated data or reversing score sign hides real anomalies. Inspect score direction and review labeled examples.

### Practice

Evaluate precision at a fixed daily alert budget using labeled anomalies.

Acceptance: Report false positives and sensitivity to changing baseline behavior.
